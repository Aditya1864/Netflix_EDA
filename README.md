# Netflix Content Catalog - Exploratory Data Analysis

This project presents an end-to-end Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) of the Netflix Titles dataset using Python. The analysis focuses on understanding Netflix's content catalog through data preprocessing, exploratory analysis, and visualization to identify trends in content type, ratings, genres, release years, production countries, directors, and actors.

## Technologies Used

- Python
- Pandas
- NumPy
- Matplotlib
- Seaborn
- Google Colab

## Project Highlights

- Analyzed **8,807 Netflix titles** through data cleaning, preprocessing, and exploratory data analysis.
- Explored catalog trends across content type, ratings, genres, release years, countries, directors, and actors.
- Generated business insights and recommendations to support content acquisition and regional expansion strategies.

## Key Insights

- **Movies dominate the Netflix catalog**, accounting for **6,131 titles (69.6%)**, while **TV Shows comprise 2,676 titles (30.4%)**.
- **TV-MA (3,207 titles)** and **TV-14 (2,160 titles)** are the most common content ratings, indicating a strong focus on mature audiences.
- Netflix experienced rapid catalog expansion between **2016 and 2019**, with **2019 recording the highest number of content additions (1,999 titles)**.
- The **United States (3,690 titles)** is the largest content-producing country, followed by **India (1,046)** and the **United Kingdom (806)**.
- **International Movies (2,752 titles)** and **Dramas (2,427 titles)** are the most prevalent genres, reflecting Netflix's emphasis on global and dramatic content.

## Recommendations

- Continue investing in **movie content** while strategically expanding high-quality **TV Shows** to maintain a balanced catalog.
- Strengthen content partnerships in key production markets such as the **United States, India, and the United Kingdom** while exploring emerging regional markets.
- Increase investments in **International Movies** and **Drama** genres, which represent the largest share of the catalog.
- Improve metadata quality for **directors and cast** to enhance searchability and recommendation systems.
- Optimize annual content acquisition by leveraging historical trends, particularly around periods of high catalog expansion.
